[30] Chapter 12: Marketing – Market Research
[31] Main topics:
[32] What is marketing: identifying and satisfying customer needs profitably.
[33] Primary market research (field research):
[34] Questionnaires
[35] Interviews
[36] Focus groups
[37] Observation
[38] Advantages: specific and up-to-date information.
[39] Disadvantages: expensive and time-consuming.
[40] Secondary market research (desk research):
[41] Existing information such as CSO data, industry reports, and competitor information.
[42] Advantages: cheaper and faster.
[43] Disadvantages: information may be outdated or not specific enough.

[45] Data analysis: qualitative and quantitative data.
[46] Research ethics: privacy, informed consent, and responsible use of data.

[48] Market research reduces the risk of launching products that customers do not want.
[49] Combining primary and secondary research provides a more complete picture of the market.
[50] Data should be reliable, representative, and up to date.

[91] Main topics:
[92] Cash flow vs profit: the difference between liquidity and profitability.
[93] Cash flow forecast: forecasts of monthly cash inflows and outflows.
[94] Causes of cash-flow problems:
[95] Bad debts
[96] Excess stock
[97] Investment in fixed assets
[98] Seasonal demand
[99] Strategies to improve cash flow:
[100] Collecting payments faster
[101] Offering discounts for early payment
[102] Delaying payments to suppliers
[103] Reducing stock levels
[104] Leasing instead of purchasing assets
[105] Budgets: financial plans for a future period.
[106] Types of budgets: sales, production, cash, and investment budgets.
[107] Monitoring: comparing budgeted figures with actual results and carrying out variance analysis.
[108] Key concepts:
[109] A business can be profitable but still fail because it does not have enough cash.
[110] A cash-flow forecast can help prevent cash shortages.
[111] Budgets help businesses plan and control their performance.
